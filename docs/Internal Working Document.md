# WE3  
## Operating Model, Rationale, and Learning System  
**Internal Working Document**

---

## 1. Purpose of this document

This document exists to make WE3 governable.

Its role is to:
- capture the reasoning that led to the WE3 model,
- make assumptions, constraints, and trade-offs explicit,
- guide decisions when priorities conflict,
- preserve institutional memory as the organisation evolves.

This is not a manifesto and not a strategy deck.  
It is a **design justification and operating reference**.

When decisions feel unclear, this is the document to return to.

---

## 2. The problem space WE3 is designed for

WE3 is designed to operate in situations characterised by:

- high uncertainty (the right problem is not yet fully known),
- cross-disciplinary dependency (product, design, technology, and business trade-offs are inseparable),
- meaningful downside risk (time, capital, reputation),
- and pressure for real outcomes, not analysis alone.

Typical contexts include:
- new venture creation or incubation,
- complex product or platform builds,
- transformation work where execution quality determines strategy viability.

WE3 is **not** designed for:
- high-volume, low-variance delivery,
- compliance-driven programmes,
- work where scale and leverage matter more than judgment.

This boundary is intentional.

---

## 3. Core assumptions and constraints

### 3.1 Market assumptions

We currently assume that:

- Demand is growing for senior-led, outcome-focused delivery in complex problem contexts.
- Clients increasingly resist leverage-heavy consulting models where accountability is diffuse.
- Generative AI reduces the value of junior throughput work while increasing the premium on judgment, integration, and decision quality.
- Private equity–backed and founder-led organisations are earlier adopters of alternative delivery models than large bureaucratic enterprises.

**Confidence level:** medium–high  
**Risk:** demand may polarise rather than uniformly shift; WE3 must remain selective.

---

### 3.2 Delivery assumptions

We assume that:

- Decision latency is a primary driver of failure in complex initiatives.
- Handoffs between strategy, design, and delivery materially increase rework and delay.
- Small, senior, cross-functional teams outperform larger teams on ambiguous work.
- Accountability must sit with those closest to the work.

**Confidence level:** high  
**Risk:** small teams can fail through overconfidence, groupthink, or capacity limits.

---

### 3.3 Organisational constraints

We accept the following constraints as non-negotiable:

- WE3 will not scale primarily through headcount growth.
- Senior practitioners must remain directly accountable for outcomes.
- Capacity will be capped deliberately.
- Some opportunities will be declined to preserve integrity of delivery.

These constraints define WE3’s shape.

---

## 4. The WE3 design response

### 4.1 Small teams as a structural choice

Small teams are not a cost choice; they are a **decision-quality choice**.

Benefits:
- reduced coordination overhead,
- faster sense-making,
- clearer ownership,
- earlier surfacing of trade-offs.

Trade-offs:
- limited parallelism,
- constrained scalability,
- dependence on individual capability.

We accept these trade-offs deliberately.

---

### 4.2 The triad model

The core WE3 delivery unit is a **triad**:
- product leadership,
- design leadership,
- engineering leadership.

The triad exists to collapse decisions.

Each member:
- has equal authority,
- owns outcomes collectively,
- is accountable beyond their functional discipline.

The triad is treated as a **decision-making system**, not a role hierarchy.

---

#### Failure modes and mitigations

Known risks:
- groupthink,
- dominance by one discipline,
- decision paralysis.

Mitigations:
- explicit decision cadence,
- external challenge and review,
- fractional specialist input when depth is required.

---

## 5. Engagement modes and portfolio logic

WE3 operates across three modes. They form a **single portfolio**, not three businesses.

### 5.1 Consulting

Purpose:
- deliver immediate client value,
- surface real-world problems,
- generate cashflow.

Characteristics:
- senior-led delivery,
- hands-on execution,
- clear accountability.

Consulting work always takes priority over internal ventures.

---

### 5.2 Co-building

Purpose:
- align incentives around outcomes,
- build ventures with strong partners,
- extend WE3’s impact beyond advisory work.

Characteristics:
- shared delivery responsibility,
- fee + equity or revenue participation,
- explicit governance and decision rights.

Co-building is selective, not default.

---

### 5.3 Internal venture creation

Purpose:
- act on repeatable opportunity patterns,
- retain exceptional talent,
- compound learning over time.

Constraints:
- venture capacity is capped,
- ventures are reviewed against explicit progress criteria,
- ventures are terminated when learning stalls.

Internal ventures must never silently tax consulting delivery.

---

## 6. Governance and prioritisation rules

When priorities conflict, the following rules apply:

1. **Active client delivery takes precedence.**
2. **Capacity is finite and visible.**
3. **Ventures must justify continued investment.**
4. **Senior accountability cannot be delegated away.**

If these rules cannot be honoured, the work should not be taken on.

---

## 7. Go-to-market logic

WE3’s GTM strategy is based on **clarity-first engagement**.

Assumptions:
- Many engagements fail due to poor problem framing.
- Providing value before a contract builds trust.
- Reducing scoping ambiguity improves delivery outcomes.

Tools and methods (e.g. brief builders) serve to:
- help prospects articulate real problems,
- qualify fit early,
- reduce sales and delivery friction.

The goal is not lead volume; it is alignment quality.

---

## 8. Talent, learning, and sustainability

WE3 treats talent development as a system design problem.

Principles:
- senior practitioners remain accountable,
- learning is structured and supervised,
- junior contribution does not dilute client outcomes.

We accept slower growth in exchange for:
- depth of capability,
- resilience of the model,
- long-term talent compounding.

---

## 9. Risk model

### Key risks
- overcommitment and burnout,
- dilution of senior attention,
- venture distraction,
- drift toward conventional consulting under pressure.

### Early warning signals
- increasing utilisation pressure,
- reduced senior presence in delivery,
- blurred venture governance,
- acceptance of misaligned work.

When these appear, intervention is required.

---

## 10. Learning loops and evolution

WE3 is a hypothesis-driven organisation.

We track:
- delivery cycle time and outcomes,
- client repeat engagement,
- venture progression and failure rates,
- talent retention and progression.

Assumptions must be revisited if:
- delivery quality declines,
- client trust erodes,
- the venture portfolio consumes disproportionate attention,
- market signals shift materially.

This document should evolve as evidence accumulates.

---

## 11. What success looks like internally

WE3 is succeeding if:
- decisions are made close to the work,
- small teams consistently create momentum under uncertainty,
- failure happens early and cheaply,
- the organisation remains selective rather than reactive.

Growth is a consequence, not a goal.

---

## 12. Status of this document

This is a living artefact.

It should be:
- reviewed periodically,
- challenged when reality diverges,
- updated deliberately rather than drifted away from.

If WE3 ever feels like it is “losing its way”, this document should explain why.

---

### End note

WE3 is not optimised for comfort, certainty, or scale.

It is optimised for judgment, responsibility, and learning under constraint.

This document exists to keep that optimisation explicit.
